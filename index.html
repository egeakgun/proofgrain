Evaluation protocol & metric definitions +

Protocol

18 private mathematics problems, one no-hint attempt per model and problem. Each attempt receives up to 180 seconds for solving and up to 30 seconds for a same-model final write-up when needed. No external tools are available. The requested output ceiling is 32,768 tokens.

GPT-6 Astra grades submissions against reference solutions and explicit 0–7 rubrics. Model identities are provider-reported labels through a Devin inference adapter. Requests were recorded on November 7–8, 2026, Pacific time.

Reading the results

Full credit: valid 7/7 submitted responses divided by all 18 assigned attempts. Proof score: the mean of graded final submissions. Grading coverage is shown separately; reasoning-only captures and invalid or missing grades are excluded from the mean.

Uncertainty: 95% bootstrap intervals over scored problems, using 1,000 resamples. One attempt per problem does not estimate repeat variability. Intervals do not account for task selection or grading error. Elapsed time: provider, network, and generation time, including unsuccessful attempts and excluding queue waiting.

02 / OUR VISION

Scientific expertise should
produce reusable systems.

Building scientific rl datasets, benchmarks, and training methods.

Starting with mathematics and will expand into scientific domains.

Our approach takes a lesson from Bitter Lesson: learning and search can keep improving with more computation, while systems built around hand-designed human knowledge eventually reach limits.

For Proofgrain, the implication is that scientific expertise should produce reusable systems. We want the work of an expert to keep producing training environments.

This requires high-quality data. We believe calling data “expert-generated” doesn't establish its correctness or usefulness.

Our datasets contain ranging from original tasks to model failure explanations.

Alongside these datasets, we will develop and release public benchmarks with clear evaluation methods.

Our research team will use difficult tasks to identify capability gaps and guide new training material. We will keep training tasks separate from evaluation tasks to test whether improvements are real.

We believe this combination can improve reinforcement learning research and help smaller models, including models around 30 billion parameters.

A central research direction is logical reliability over extended scientific work. The findings will guide training data and feedback that help models maintain consistency and correct their reasoning.

Right now we will sell scientific rl tasks and eval projects to model developers. We will build recurring data partnerships, task environments, and research collaborations. Customer needs and observed model failures will guide our research; successful research will improve the data, evaluations, and training systems we provide.

03 / LONG-TERM GOALS

A research agenda
with practical consequences.

  1. Become a trusted source of scientific training data.

    Expand from mathematics into scientific domains where we can establish credible verification methods and recruit the necessary expertise.

  2. Continually create frontier challenges.

    Develop original tasks, benchmarks, and generation systems that remain useful as model capabilities advance.

  3. Improve reasoning over extended tasks.

    Understand how logical errors emerge and accumulate, and develop data and feedback that help models maintain consistency, revise assumptions, and recover from mistakes.

  4. Research how models learn scientific capabilities.

    Study how data selection, task difficulty, curricula, and feedback affect midtraining, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning. Measure transfer to unfamiliar problems, reliability over longer runs, and improvement relative to training cost.

  5. Connect research with commercial value.

    Use customer needs and model failures to guide research, then turn successful research into better datasets, environments, and training partnerships.

04 / THE NEXT SIX MONTHS

From a working foundation
to validated datasets.

Our goals for the next six months.

  1. Produce the first validated mathematical datasets.

    Complete reviewable samples and develop them into datasets suitable for customer pilots, with provenance, reference solutions, error annotations, corrections, and verification records.

  2. Establish the generation and verification pipeline.

    Build reusable tools for collecting model attempts, generating task variants, checking solutions, coordinating independent review, and tracking defects. Keep training data and evaluation tasks separate.

  3. Develop an initial frontier benchmark.

    Select a focused set of mathematical or scientific tasks, including tasks requiring consistency across multiple dependent steps. Measure performance and investigate error introduction, propagation, and recovery.

  4. Secure the first paid pilots.

    Work with model-training teams to identify capability gaps, agree on acceptance criteria, and evaluate our data. Use the results to shape recurring offerings.

  5. Run controlled training experiments.

    Compare our data against strong baselines on accessible models, beginning with supervised fine-tuning and testing RL where reliable rewards are available. Use the findings to define a concrete midtraining research agenda.

  6. Establish the core research team.

    Bring together complementary expertise in mathematics, verification, model training, and systems engineering. The team will own dataset quality and the research needed to create increasingly difficult, useful tasks.
